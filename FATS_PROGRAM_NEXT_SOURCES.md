# Fats & Oils program: sources still worth finding

Written 2026-10-25 after the overnight pass. Everything below is a gap that the books already in the Food History folder cannot close. Each item says what it would fill, which entry or overview flags the gap, and a verdict: **GET** (worth finding), **ALT** (an alternative you already hold, or a free one), or **LIVE WITHOUT** (I would stop here).

Titles are ones I am confident exist. Where I am not sure a book covers the point, I say so. The program's rule stands: anything I add from a web source stays tiered SECONDARY or UNVERIFIED until you have the book.

## 2. Regional holes in the overviews

### Near East, 1500–1900 (no entry in three folios except one Syrian manual of about 1892)
- **GET** Beshara Doumani, *Rediscovering Palestine: Merchants and Peasants in Jabal Nablus, 1700–1900* (University of California Press, 1995). Nablus soap and olive oil are the heart of its economy. This would give `fats-1750-1850` and `fats-1850-1900` a real Ottoman entry.
- **GET (maybe)** Bruce Masters, *The Origins of Western Economic Dominance in the Middle East: Mercantilism and the Islamic Economy in Aleppo, 1600–1750* (1988). I am not sure how much it says on soap and oil; check the index for "soap" and "olive" before spending time.
- **LIVE WITHOUT** a Safavid entry; I know no standard book that treats fats there.

### Africa, 600–1750
- **GET** Richard Pankhurst, *An Economic History of Ethiopia, 1800–1935* (1968): butter, oilseeds and noug exports. It would also seed the Ethiopia gap in `fats-600-1000` (only as later evidence, so I would date it honestly).
- **GET** Jack Goody, *Cooking, Cuisine and Class* (1982): compares West African and Eurasian fats (shea, palm oil, butter). Check the index for "shea" and "oil".
- **ALT** Dahl & Hjort, *Having Herds* (1976), cited in Kiple's camel chapter, for pastoral milk fat in East Africa; the Kiple entry already carries the main figures.
- **LIVE WITHOUT** a Swahili-coast fat entry unless a source turns up; I know of none.

### Americas, before 1500 and colonial
- **GET** Sophie Coe, *America's First Cuisines* (University of Texas Press, 1994): Aztec, Maya and Inca food, including how little fat they had and what they used. It would fill the Mexico and Andes holes in `fats-1000-1500` and `fats-1500-1750`, and likely the Maya/Moche-era gap below too.
- **ALT** Kiple's introduction (held) already gives the "few herds, tree fats" argument; it is now `fats-8000bc-1200bc::th6`.
- **GET (Maya, Classic period, 300 BC–1000 AD)**: Christine White's isotope/dietary papers on the Maya (with Fred Longstaffe), or the Florida Museum's Maya lipid-residue project (floridamuseum.ufl.edu/envarch/research/maya) — only their project page was read, not their actual papers; a name that surfaced was Lisa Duffy.
- **GET (Andes, Moche era, 300 BC–1000 AD)**: Nicolas Goepfert's own papers, especially the 2014 *Antiquity* piece on the Sechura Desert marine-processing site — only abstracts were found, not the papers. Daniel Sandweiss on Peruvian coastal archaeology is another name worth checking, not yet verified against anything read. Moseley himself (now read) stops around 1000 BC, so he doesn't reach this later window.

### Far East
- **GET** Susan Hanley, *Everyday Things in Premodern Japan* (1997) for lamp oil and cooking oil in Edo Japan; and Kalland & Moeran, *Japanese Whaling: End of an Era?* (1992) for Japanese whaling after Taiji.
- **ALT** Sung Ying-hsing, *T'ien-kung k'ai-wu*, translated by E-tu Zen Sun and Shiou-chuan Sun as *Chinese Technology in the Seventeenth Century* (1966): the primary text for Ming oil pressing. Needham 6.5 (held) already summarises it, so this is only to check my reading.
- **GET (uncertain titles)** K. T. Achaya's other books. The dust jacket of the held *Indian Food Tradition* says he wrote on the history of Indian oilseeds, traditional oilseed technology and the food industries of the British period; I could not confirm the titles, and they are the best route to India 1750–1900.
- **GET (partial)** Irfan Habib, *The Agrarian System of Mughal India* (1963) for oilseed crops in the Mughal economy; I am not sure how much it gives on oils.

### Europe
- **GET** the Roman agronomists and Pliny in translation: Cato, *De agricultura*; Columella, *De re rustica*; Pliny, *Natural History* book 15 (olive oil). All are in the Loeb series and free online (Bill Thayer's LacusCurtius has Pliny). This closes the "Roman literary sources were not read" gap in `fats-300bc-600ad`.
- **GET (maybe)** Ibn al-'Awwam, *Kitab al-filaha* (twelfth-century Seville), in J.-J. Clément-Mullet's French translation (1864–67): olive and oil chapters. This is the best stand-in for the Watson gap.
- **GET** Gordon Jackson, *The British Whaling Trade* (1978) for Hull, Whitby and the Greenland fishery; it would add depth to the English side in `fats-1500-1750` and `fats-1750-1850`.
- For medieval Italian, Spanish and Provençal oil and soap trade (a flagged gap in `fats-1000-1500`), I know no single book to name with confidence. If you tell me which of Italian, Spanish, French or Catalan you can read, I can search for articles in that language.

### Pacific
- No title I can name with confidence for the nineteenth-century Pacific coconut-oil and copra trade (Godeffroy at Samoa, Tahiti, Fiji). The search terms to try in the *Journal of Pacific History* are "copra" and "coconut oil trade", with "Godeffroy". Until then the Pacific enters only through Ellis, Dolin, Kirch and the Lever material.

### Egypt
- **GET (optional)** Serpico & White, "Oil, fat and wax", in Nicholson & Shaw (eds), *Ancient Egyptian Materials and Technology* (Cambridge, 2000). It would update Lucas (1959) on Egyptian oils. **LIVE WITHOUT** if it is hard to find; Lucas is thorough for the question.

### Nutrition science, 1950–today
- **ALT, free** American Heart Association presidential advisory *Dietary Fats and Cardiovascular Disease* (Sacks et al., *Circulation*, 2017), and the WHO 2015 guideline on saturated and trans fatty acid intake. They give the mainstream side that Teicholz and Taubes attack.
- **GET (optional)** Ancel Keys, *Seven Countries* (1980).
